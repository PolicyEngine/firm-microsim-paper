\begin{abstract}
\noindent I present an open firm-level microsimulation for conditional costing
of UK VAT registration-threshold reforms. A weighted synthetic population is
calibrated to ONS and HMRC aggregates on two declared universes, and
level, taper and reduced-rate scenarios are costed on a common base. With
membership evaluated on data-year turnover, a mechanical costing of the April
2024 rise from \pounds85{,}000 to \pounds90{,}000 lies within a third of HMRC's
published profile in each release year with no tuned parameter. The notch's
dominated region is \pounds21{,}250 for a firm with no reclaimable inputs but,
once unregistered firms bear input VAT, firm-specific and zero for about half
of near-threshold firms; a reduced-rate band then creates a distortion where
none existed. Intensive-margin responses are second order. The near-threshold
density is target-inherited, so the data cannot support bunching inference.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{0.3cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} value-added tax, microsimulation, tax notch, registration threshold
