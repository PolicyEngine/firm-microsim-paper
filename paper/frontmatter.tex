\begin{abstract}
I present an open, reproducible firm-level microsimulation for costing UK VAT registration-threshold reforms, released with its synthetic-data generator. The threshold is a \emph{notch}: once turnover crosses \pounds85{,}000, VAT falls due on a firm's whole base. I build synthetic firm records calibrated to 2023--24 HMRC and OBR statistics, and cost \emph{level}, \emph{shape} (a taper) and \emph{rate} (a reduced-rate band) reforms statically on a common \pounds184.8bn base: raising the threshold to \pounds100{,}000 costs \pounds753m, a taper \pounds520m, and reduced-rate bands \pounds484m and \pounds242m. I characterise the notch by its \emph{dominated region}---the \pounds21{,}250 band above the threshold in which no firm profits from locating---and how each reform changes it. Conditional on an assumed elasticity, intensive-margin responses barely move these costings. The near-threshold shape is target-inherited: a placebo regenerated without the OBR targets returns zero excess mass, so band-calibrated data support no bunching inference.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{0.3cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} value-added tax, microsimulation, tax notch, registration threshold
