\begin{abstract}
\noindent I present an open firm-level microsimulation for transparent, conditional
costing of UK VAT registration-threshold reforms. The model generates a
weighted accounting population from public aggregates; it does not recover
administrative microdata or firm behaviour. On a common 2023--24 baseline, I
apply \emph{level}, \emph{shape} (a taper), and \emph{rate} (a reduced-rate
band) scenarios and report their mechanical revenue effects. I characterise
the notch by its model-implied \emph{dominated region}---a
\pounds21{,}250 band above the \pounds85{,}000 threshold---and show how each
schedule changes it. Intensive-margin results are sensitivity exercises
conditional on a region-confined response model and assumed elasticities; the
extensive margin is not identified. The near-threshold shape is
target-inherited, and specification diagnostics show that the synthetic
data cannot support bunching inference. The exercise therefore supplies reproducible policy scenarios
and a general warning: calibrated synthetic data cannot create behavioural or
sub-band fiscal information absent from their targets and maintained
assumptions.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{0.3cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} value-added tax, microsimulation, tax notch, registration threshold
