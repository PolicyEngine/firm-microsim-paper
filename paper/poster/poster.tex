  \begin{alertblock}{The problem: VAT as a tax \emph{notch}}
    The UK VAT registration threshold is a \hl{notch}, not a kink. Once
    taxable turnover crosses $T^\ast$, \hl{20\% VAT falls due on the
    \emph{whole} turnover}, not just the increment --- a discrete liability
    jump of \hl{$\tau T^\ast=$~\pounds17{,}000} at $T^\ast=$~\pounds85{,}000.
    Threshold \hl{\pounds85{,}000} (2023--24 data year), raised to
    \hl{\pounds90{,}000} in April 2024 --- among the \hl{highest in the
    OECD} --- and it sits in a \emph{dense} part of the firm-size
    distribution. Firms respond by \hl{locating just below} it; the cost of
    crossing is sharpest for \emph{consumer-facing} firms with little
    reclaimable input VAT, so the notch distorts both the size distribution
    and firms' growth at the margin.
  \end{alertblock}

  \begin{figure}
    \centering
    {\itshape VAT/PAYE-registered businesses by turnover band --- ONS, 2023--24}\\[0.4em]
    \includegraphics[width=0.70\colwidth]{figures/firms_by_turnover_band_85k.png}
  \end{figure}

  \vfill

  \begin{block}{Three reform families, one basis}
    \leadin{What we cost.}
    Costed on a \emph{common static basis}:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \hl{Level} --- move the threshold up or down.
      \item \hl{Shape} --- a graduated taper that phases VAT in.
      \item \hl{Rate} --- a reduced-rate band above the threshold.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{block}

  \vfill

  \begin{alertblock}{Contribution}
    {\color{peteal}\bfseries Headline: only the graduated taper removes the
      notch's dominated region (\pounds550m, vs \pounds497m for a 10\% band
      that removes none); a level rise costs most --- and has exactly zero
      intensive-margin behavioural offset.}\par\vspace*{0.45em}
    An open, reproducible firm-level microsimulation that:
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item costs \hl{level + shape + rate} reforms on one static basis;
      \item characterises the notch's distortion via its exact,
            \hl{elasticity-free dominated region};
      \item proves the near-threshold shape is \hl{target-inherited}
            (OBR-calibrated; placebo-verified) --- in both directions;
      \item adds a conditional \hl{behavioural layer} with an exact
            static nesting.
    \end{enumerate}
  \end{alertblock}

  \vfill

  \begin{exampleblock}{Data methodology}
    \leadin{How the synthetic data are built.}
    Draw synthetic firms by sector\,$\times$\,turnover band; give each an
    input share $x_i=\rho_i y_i$ and net VAT liability
    $v_i=0.20\,(y_i-x_i)$ --- the standard rate on value added. Firm weights
    $w_i=e^{\theta_i}$ are fit by gradient descent to HMRC + ONS aggregates,
    with the near-threshold \pounds1k-band profile pinned to the
    \hl{OBR's published Chart~C data}. The threshold is a \emph{generator
    parameter} --- every counterfactual \hl{regenerates} a fresh
    population --- and the whole file is \hl{released openly}.
  \end{exampleblock}

  \vfill

\end{column}

  \begin{exampleblock}{Static costing}
    \leadin{Pricing a reform on this data.}
    \[ R(T)=\sum_i w_i\, v_i\,\mathds{1}\{y_i\ge T\} \]
    Revenue moves only through firms whose registration status flips.\\[0.4em]
    \hl{Anchor vs HMRC:} the \pounds85k$\to$\pounds90k April-2024 rise, under
    two released-firm conventions, \hl{overshoots early} ($-$\pounds366m /
    $-$\pounds209m retention vs published $-$\pounds185m in 2025--26 ---
    consistent with HMRC's phased registration response) and
    \hl{matches by 2026--27} ($-$\pounds125.1m vs $-$\pounds125m), with the
    2028--29 sign flip reproduced.
  \end{exampleblock}

    \centering

    \leadin{Scaling the \emph{level} reform across thresholds.}

  \begin{block}{The near-threshold shape is target-inherited}
    \leadin{A transparency result, proved in both directions.}
    The 2023--24 file reproduces the administrative bunching profile
    \hl{because it is calibrated to it} (OBR \pounds1k-band targets); the
    estimator reads back \hl{$E=7{,}873$}. Regenerate \emph{without} those
    targets $\Rightarrow$ \hl{$E=0$}. And with \emph{no} fine targets, the
    2024--25 vintage's coarse \pounds90k band edge alone yields
    \hl{$b_{\mathrm{LLAT}}=1.23$} --- within 10\% of Liu et al.'s (2021)
    administrative \hl{1.361}, coincidentally and unstably. Synthetic
    bunching is \hl{never behavioural evidence}; the administrative estimate
    remains the behavioural fact.
    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=0.74\colwidth]{figures/bunching_analysis_85k.png}
    \end{figure}
  \end{block}

  \vfill

  \begin{alertblock}{The dominated region}
    \leadin{Beyond revenue: measuring the notch's distortion.}
    Profit jumps \emph{down} by $\tau T^\ast=$~\pounds17{,}000 at the
    threshold. The \hl{dominated region} --- turnover where no firm locates
    because it earns less than at $T^\ast$ --- has exact width
    \[ a=T^\ast\tfrac{\tau}{1-\tau}=\text{\pounds}21{,}250 \]
    (\pounds85{,}000--\pounds106{,}250), spanning \hl{$\approx$160{,}000}
    weighted firms. \hl{Exact, elasticity-free, and input-share invariant}
    (the deductible share cancels in the value-added formulation).
    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=0.72\colwidth]{figures/dynamic_notch_fit_e017.png}
    \end{figure}
  \end{alertblock}

\begin{column}{\colwidth}

  \begin{block}{How reforms change it}
    \leadin{\flow Which reforms actually shrink this band?}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Raising the threshold \hl{relocates} the \pounds21{,}250 band
            unchanged.
      \item A banded reduced rate does \hl{not shrink} it. It lowers the
            \emph{largest single} notch (\pounds17{,}000$\to$\hl{\pounds12{,}750}
            at 15\%), but reverting to 20\% at the band top adds a
            \emph{secondary} notch $a'=T_1\tfrac{\tau-r}{1-\tau}$ of
            \pounds5{,}250, so total width is
            \hl{\pounds21{,}562 (15\%)} / \hl{\pounds22{,}569 (10\%)} --- it
            \hl{splits} the band, not shrinks it.
      \item \hl{Only the graduated taper removes the distortion} entirely.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{block}

  \vfill

  \begin{block}{Behavioural layer}
    \leadin{Layering an assumed response on the static costs.}
    Conditional on an \hl{assumed} turnover elasticity $e$ (\emph{not}
    identified on synthetic data); sweep $e\in\{0.05,0.17,0.32\}$. The
    intensive margin \hl{barely moves the costings}: a level rise has
    \hl{exactly zero} intensive-margin offset at every $e$ (released firms
    leave the VAT base, so their expansion is untaxed), and the reduced-rate
    offsets stay \hl{under 4\%} (10\% band: \pounds497m to \hl{\pounds488m}
    at $e=0.17$). The taper is \emph{excluded} --- its rate varies
    continuously, outside the flat-rate machinery. Only $e=0.05$ has an
    external anchor, so this is a \hl{sensitivity range}, not a forecast ---
    and the $e\to0$ limit reproduces the static costs \hl{exactly}.
    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=0.80\colwidth]{figures/dynamic_cost_vs_elasticity.png}
    \end{figure}
  \end{block}

  \vfill

  \begin{alertblock}{Conclusions}
    {\color{peteal}\bfseries What this paper is:} an open, reproducible
    microsimulation that models the UK VAT threshold as a
    \emph{turnover-tax notch} on synthetic firm data, and prices level, shape
    and rate reforms three ways --- statically, by the exact distortion each
    removes, and behaviourally.\par\vspace*{0.5em}
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item Prices level + shape + rate on one basis; tracks HMRC's anchor
            costing once the deregistration convention is stated.
      \item On the dominated-region metric, \hl{only the taper removes the
            distortion}; the level move costs most and removes none of it.
      \item Sub-band structure in calibrated data is \hl{target-inherited} ---
            never behavioural evidence, in either direction.
      \item The intensive margin barely moves the static costings; the static
            cost and dominated region are the \hl{elasticity-free anchors}.
    \end{enumerate}
  \end{alertblock}
