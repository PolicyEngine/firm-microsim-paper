\section{Bunching and the limits of band-calibrated synthetic data}
\label{sec:bunching}

This section applies the polynomial counterfactual-density estimator of
\citet{chettyetal2011} and \citet{klevenwaseem2013}, as used for UK VAT by
\citet{liuetal2021}, to the synthetic population. Its finding is about the
data, not about firms: every sub-band feature of a band-calibrated synthetic
population is what its targets put there, so the excess mass the estimator
reads at \pounds85{,}000 is the OBR profile the population was calibrated to.

\subsection{Methodology}

Firms are binned in \pounds1{,}000 intervals and a counterfactual density
$g(y)$ is fitted by polynomial regression on bin counts outside a manipulation
window around the threshold $y^{*}$, then projected into the window
(Appendix~\ref{app:inference} gives the specification). The upper edge of the
window, $y_R$, is located by integrating missing mass upward from the
threshold until it equals the excess mass below; where the missing mass inside
the search window falls short, $y_R$ is censored at the window edge and
reported as such. Excess mass is the gross positive-part gap
\[
  E \;=\; \int_{y^{*}-W}^{y^{*}} \bigl[f(y)-g(y)\bigr]_{+}\,\mathrm{d}y ,
\]
reported alongside its signed net counterpart. The normalised excess
$b_{\mathrm{LLAT}}$ divides the positive-part gap over $[y^{*}-W,\,y_R]$ by the
average counterfactual height over that region, in the spirit of
\citet{liuetal2021} without reproducing their estimator exactly. No elasticity
is computed: the identification critique of bunching designs applies
\citep{blomquistetal2021,bertanhaetal2023}, and on these data there is no
behavioural mass to convert.

\subsection{Results: diagnostics, not behavioural estimates}

At the \pounds85{,}000 threshold (Figure~\ref{fig:bunch85}) the baseline
specification, run on the OBR chart's own universe (frame plus unregistered
stratum below the threshold, frame alone above it), returns a gross excess
mass of $E=22{,}407$ weighted firms, a signed net excess of $4{,}431$, a
fitted missing mass of $\Delta_R=22{,}700$ with an interior marginal buncher
at $y_R=\pounds93{,}200$, and $b_{\mathrm{LLAT}}=1.513$. Mass conservation
binds and the normalised excess sits next to LLAT's administrative $1.361$;
neither fact is behavioural. The profile the estimator reads is the OBR
\pounds1{,}000-band profile the population was calibrated to, the generator
contains no location choice, and the statistics are unstable across the
specification grid ($E$ from zero to about $186{,}200$;
$b_{\mathrm{LLAT}}$ from zero to $19.9$). The universe matters: on all rows,
including the stratum's exempt tail above the threshold that the chart does
not count, the same specification gives $E=8{,}268$ with a negative net
excess and a censored $y_R$; on in-scope firms alone it gives $E=38{,}873$
(Appendix~\ref{app:inference}). The 2024--25 population, which has no
fine-band targets, makes the complementary point: at its \pounds90{,}000
threshold the estimator returns $E=30{,}807$ and $b_{\mathrm{LLAT}}=2.262$,
produced by the step between the two HMRC coarse bands that meet at
\pounds90{,}000, which the weight optimiser resolves as a density step; that
step is why the $\pm\pounds5{,}000$ rows of Table~\ref{tab:static_revenue}
are flagged as conditional on the within-band allocation. Appendix~\ref{app:inference} reports two constructed-data exercises that
characterise the estimator itself.

\paragraph{Comparison with the literature.} \citet{liuetal2021} (hereafter
LLAT) document genuine bunching at the UK VAT notch in HMRC micro-data over
2004--05 to 2014--15, with an excess-bunching ratio of $b = 1.361$ (SE
$0.202$) and a manipulation window of $-\pounds14{,}000$ to
$+\pounds24{,}000$. The synthetic $b_{\mathrm{LLAT}}=1.513$ is not a second
estimate of their parameter: it is a readback of the OBR target profile. What does carry across is institutional context:
LLAT report that about $43\%$ of eligible below-threshold firms register
voluntarily, which motivates the voluntary-retention sensitivity of
Section~\ref{sec:static}.
