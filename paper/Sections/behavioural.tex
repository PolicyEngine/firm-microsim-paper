\section{Behavioural reform costs}
\label{sec:behavioural}

The costings of Section~\ref{sec:static} hold turnover fixed. This section
adds an intensive-margin response for an \emph{assumed} turnover elasticity
$e$, reported as a sensitivity range that nests the static costing exactly at
$e\to0$. Its result is deflationary: on the intensive margin the static
numbers are robust.

\subsection{An iso-elastic response, region-confined}
\label{ssec:simulator}

Following \citet{klevenwaseem2013} and \citet{saez2010}, a firm of ability
$n_i$ chooses turnover $y$ to maximise
\begin{equation}
  \pi_i(y) \;=\; (1-\delta_i)\,\bigl(1-\tau\,f(y)\bigr)\,y \;-\;
  C(y;n_i,e), \qquad
  C(y;n,e)=\frac{n}{1+1/e}\left(\frac{y}{n}\right)^{1+1/e},
  \label{eq:isoelastic}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_i$ is the input share, $f(y)\in[0,1]$ the fraction of the
statutory rate the schedule levies at $y$, and $C$ the own-factor cost (bought-in
inputs are already netted out through $1-\delta_i$). Under a flat rate the
optimum is
\begin{equation}
  y^{*} \;=\; n\,\bigl[(1-\delta)(1-\tau)\bigr]^{e},
  \label{eq:flat-optimum}
\end{equation}
so $e$ is the elasticity of turnover with respect to the retained wedge. Each
firm re-optimises \emph{within the region of the reform schedule that contains
its observed turnover}, taking that turnover as optimal under the baseline
notch; inverting~\eqref{eq:flat-optimum} at the baseline fraction $f_0$ and
re-solving at the reform fraction $f_1$, ability and input share cancel:
\begin{equation}
  y^{*}_i \;=\; y_{\mathrm{obs},i}\,
  \left(\frac{1-\tau f_1}{1-\tau f_0}\right)^{e},
  \label{eq:rescale}
\end{equation}
clipped at the region's edges. Relocation across a notch is the extensive
margin, which the synthetic data cannot identify (Section~\ref{sec:bunching})
and which Section~\ref{sec:model} characterises analytically; it is out of
scope here, and confining the response to the firm's own region is what makes
the $e\to0$ limit collapse onto the static costing exactly. The ratio is the
same under the input-VAT formulation of Section~\ref{sec:model}, because a firm
that stays in its region reclaims (or bears) input VAT on both sides of the
comparison; only the marginal buncher $n_H(e,\delta)$ differs between the
two, and under the input-VAT formulation it falls in $\delta$
(\pounds127{,}400, \pounds124{,}900 and \pounds116{,}800 at $e=0.17$ for
$\delta=0,0.2,0.4$) and does not exist for $\delta\ge\tfrac12$
(\texttt{results/dynamic\_reform\_results.txt}). The elasticity is not
identified from these data; the sweep $e\in\{0.05,0.17,0.32\}$ spans the range
used in the threshold literature, with $0.05$ the \citet{klevenwaseem2013}
anchor. \citet{liuetal2021} deliberately do not convert their bunching
estimate into a turnover elasticity, so no published UK VAT-base elasticity
anchors the sweep.

\subsection{Results}
\label{ssec:behavioural-results}

Table~\ref{tab:behavioural_costs} reports the behavioural cost of the three
flat-rate reforms at each $e$ alongside the static figure. Two features
follow directly from the region-confined solve. A level rise has \emph{no}
intensive-margin offset at any $e$: released firms expand by $(1-\tau)^{-e}$
(about $+3.9\%$ at $e=0.17$) but have left the VAT base, so the loss is their
full baseline remittance; firms above the new threshold face $f=1$ before and
after; and the only candidates for a taxed response---released firms whose
expansion would carry them across the new notch---are clipped at it by
construction. The invariance is therefore a property of the maintained status
convention, not a computed statement about global optima. A reduced-rate band
scales band firms up by~\eqref{eq:rescale} with $f_1=r/\tau$ (about $+2.0\%$
at the $10\%$ rate and $e=0.17$), an offset of about \pounds8m against a
static \pounds398m, reaching \pounds14m at $e=0.32$; Figure~\ref{fig:reform_dist}
traces the costs across $e$. The released code asserts on every run that the
baseline solve reproduces observed turnover, that the $e\to0$ cost equals the
static cost to \pounds0.1m, and that the solved response satisfies
$\mathrm{d}\ln y^{*}/\mathrm{d}\ln(1-\tau f)=e$.

The intensive-margin layer is a robustness statement: naming an elasticity
barely moves the static numbers, not at all for a level move and by under
$6\%$ for the reduced-rate bands. The behavioural uncertainty that could move
these costings materially sits on the extensive margin---registration,
deregistration and location choice at the notch---which this paper prices only
through the analytic geometry of Section~\ref{sec:model}.
